Motivation Letter
Feiyu Lu | UPC Master in Innovation and Research in Informatics

I am applying to UPC Barcelona School of Informatics' Master in Innovation and Research in Informatics because I want to strengthen the computer science foundations behind the systems I have been building professionally for nine years: distributed services, high-throughput data pipelines, low-latency financial infrastructure, and cloud observability platforms. Among UPC's programs, MIRI is especially attractive because it combines research-oriented informatics with areas directly connected to my work: advanced computing, computer networks and distributed systems, and high-performance computing.

My foundation is a B.S. in Computer Science from Tufts University, where I studied algorithms, operating systems, machine learning, security, web applications, and human-computer interaction. Industry then forced me to apply those ideas under real constraints. At Amazon Advertising, I designed Java microservices and event-driven AWS pipelines for global billing and interactive video advertising. The systems processed millions of events per day and required careful reasoning about concurrency, retries, idempotency, partial failures, monitoring, and operational reliability.

At ExodusPoint Capital Management, I worked closer to latency-sensitive decision-making. I built real-time PnL and risk infrastructure for fixed income and equities using Java, Kafka, Apache Druid, WebSocket, and Protobuf. A major technical challenge involved optimizing concurrent Java/Spring Boot services and in-memory Druid workflows that generated 100GB+ position snapshots per minute while reducing p99 query latency by 50% during market spikes. That experience made me want a more rigorous academic framework for distributed systems, performance analysis, memory behavior, and high-performance data processing.

In my current role at Amazon Web Services, I work on distributed log-ingestion and observability infrastructure. I have optimized Kinesis provisioning and ingestion throttling, implemented low-latency authorization caching, and supported observability workflows for production GenAI services. These systems are not only software projects; they are reliability problems involving architecture, measurement, failure modes, and tradeoffs between performance, cost, and correctness.

MIRI fits the next stage of my development because it would let me move from experienced production engineer toward a more complete computer scientist and systems architect. I am especially interested in the Computer Networks and Distributed Systems and High Performance Computing specializations, and in subjects such as Concurrence, Parallelism and Distributed Systems; Computer Network Architecture and Network Management; Statistical Analysis of Networks and Systems; Algorithmic Methods for Mathematical Models; and research methodology in informatics. I would like to use the master's thesis to investigate a practical problem in AI observability, financial-data infrastructure, distributed telemetry, or high-performance analytics.

My career goal is to become an architect-level technical leader who can design reliable AI, data, and cloud infrastructure for technology and financial organizations. I am drawn to UPC because of its strong CS reputation, English-taught FIB programs, Barcelona's international technology ecosystem, and the opportunity to connect rigorous academic work with a concrete builder path. I would bring MIRI a perspective shaped by production systems at Amazon, AWS, Goldman Sachs, and ExodusPoint: a habit of treating scalability, reliability, and performance not as abstract qualities, but as measurable engineering responsibilities.
