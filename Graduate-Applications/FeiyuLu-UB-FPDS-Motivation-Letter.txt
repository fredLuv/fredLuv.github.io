Motivational Letter

I am applying to the University of Barcelona's Master in Fundamental Principles of Data Science because I want to connect nine years of production engineering experience with the mathematical and algorithmic foundations behind modern data science. My career has been built around systems where data must remain correct, timely, and useful under real operational pressure: global advertising platforms, trading and risk infrastructure, and AWS cloud observability. UB's one-year, English-taught program is attractive because it treats data science as a serious intersection of computer science and mathematics, not only as a collection of tools.

My interest in data science began during my junior year at Tufts University, when I worked as a Data Scientist Intern at DigitasLBi. I used Python and Spark with TF-IDF, Word2Vec, and K-Means to cluster more than 50,000 search phrases for campaign analysis. That project showed me how raw behavioral data can become usable product and business insight when the right mathematical representation is combined with scalable computation.

Since then, my work has moved from analytical prototypes to high-scale production systems. At Amazon Advertising, I designed Java microservices and event-driven AWS pipelines supporting billing and interactive video advertising. These services processed millions of billing and telemetry events per day and required careful design around concurrency, retries, idempotency, observability, and global reliability. I learned that real data science products depend on reliable data infrastructure: the model or analysis is only as strong as the pipeline that feeds it.

At ExodusPoint Capital Management, I worked even closer to time-sensitive decision-making. I built real-time PnL and risk infrastructure for fixed income and equities using Java, Kafka, Apache Druid, WebSocket, and Protobuf. One major challenge was optimizing concurrent Java services and in-memory Druid workflows that generated 100GB+ position snapshots per minute while reducing p99 query latency by 50% during market spikes. This experience made me more interested in the principles behind optimization, numerical methods, probabilistic reasoning, and time-series analysis, because these ideas directly affect the reliability of financial analytics systems.

In my current role at Amazon Web Services, I work on distributed log ingestion and observability infrastructure. I have optimized Kinesis provisioning and ingestion throttling, implemented low-latency authorization caching, and supported AI observability workflows for production GenAI services. As AI systems become more common, the hardest engineering problems increasingly involve data quality, scalable processing, monitoring, governance, and responsible deployment.

UB's curriculum fits the next stage of my development. Courses such as Numerical Linear Algebra, Optimization, Automatic Learning, Bayesian Statistics and Probabilistic Programming, Big Data, Deep Learning, Natural Language Processing, Recommenders, and Time Series Analysis map directly to the gaps I want to close. The capstone project is also important to me: I would like to build a practical analytics or AI-infrastructure project related to financial data systems, observability, or large-scale decision support.

After the program, my goal is to become a stronger technical leader and architect for AI, data, and financial infrastructure. I want to build systems that combine rigorous algorithms with the engineering discipline required for production scale. Barcelona also offers a compelling environment for that goal: an international technology ecosystem, a reasonable cost base, and enough room to explore a serious product idea while studying. I would bring UB a production engineer's perspective, a strong computer science background from Tufts, and a clear motivation to turn data science foundations into durable systems that users can trust.
