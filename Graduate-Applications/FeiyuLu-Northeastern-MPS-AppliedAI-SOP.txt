Statement of Purpose

Feiyu (Ethan) Lu | Northeastern MPS in Applied AI

I am applying to Northeastern University's Master of Professional Studies in Applied AI because I want to connect nine years of high-scale production engineering experience with a more deliberate foundation in applied artificial intelligence. My career has already been shaped by the infrastructure that modern AI depends on: reliable data movement, distributed systems, observability, low-latency APIs, and systems that remain correct under heavy load. The next step for me is to deepen my AI knowledge and become a technical leader who can build and evaluate AI-enabled infrastructure in real business environments.

My interest in data and machine learning began during my junior year at Tufts University, when I worked as a data science intern at DigitasLBi. I used Python and Spark with TF-IDF, Word2Vec, and K-Means to cluster more than 50,000 search phrases for campaign analysis. That internship gave me an early view of how data, algorithms, and product outcomes connect. Since then, my work has moved from analytics prototypes to production systems at much larger scale.

At Amazon Advertising, I designed and launched AWS-based Java microservices and distributed event pipelines that processed millions of ad events per day. Building these services required more than writing APIs. I had to reason about concurrency, retries, idempotency, queues, observability, CI/CD, integration testing, and global reliability. It taught me that applied AI is only as useful as the systems around it: the data must arrive correctly, the pipeline must survive load, and the operational signals must be trustworthy.

At ExodusPoint Capital Management, I worked even closer to real-time decision-making. I built PnL and risk infrastructure for fixed income and equities using Java, Kafka, Apache Druid, WebSocket, and Protobuf. One major technical challenge involved optimizing concurrent Java/Spring Boot services and in-memory Druid workflows that generated 100GB position snapshots every minute while reducing p99 latency by 50% under peak load. That experience strengthened my interest in AI for finance and analytics, because the most valuable models in financial environments must coexist with strict latency, data-quality, and reliability constraints.

In my current role at Amazon AWS, I work on distributed log ingestion and observability infrastructure. I have optimized Kinesis provisioning and PutLogEvents throttling, reduced authorization latency through caching, and built a React workflow for managing API keys across CloudWatch Logs, IAM, and Secrets Manager. As AI workloads become more common across cloud platforms, observability, governance, and operational safety are becoming central technical problems. I want graduate study to help me move beyond implementation alone and think more systematically about AI system design, responsible deployment, and measurable business impact.

Northeastern's Applied AI curriculum fits this goal because it is explicitly practical and interdisciplinary. Courses such as Data Management and Big Data, Fundamentals of Artificial Intelligence, Applications of Artificial Intelligence, AI System Technologies, and Data Governance and Responsible AI match the problems I see in production: how to manage large datasets, select appropriate AI methods, build useful applications, and govern the technology responsibly. I am also drawn to the Integrated Experiential Capstone and the AI for Finance concentration, including Data Mining Applications, Finance Information Processing, Advanced Analytical Utilization, and AI Communication and Visualization. These courses would let me connect my finance and cloud background with more formal AI training.

I would bring to Northeastern a production engineer's perspective: I know how systems behave when traffic is high, users depend on them, and failures have real consequences. I also bring curiosity across domains, from advertising and cloud infrastructure to trading, analytics, and developer platforms. My goal after the program is to become a senior technical leader or architect building reliable AI, data, and cloud infrastructure for technology and financial organizations. I want to help teams move from AI demonstrations to durable systems: products that are scalable, governed, observable, and genuinely useful to decision-makers.

Northeastern's MPS in Applied AI is attractive because it values exactly that bridge between technical skill and real-world application. It would give me the structured AI foundation, applied project experience, and professional network to turn my systems background into a stronger AI infrastructure leadership path.
