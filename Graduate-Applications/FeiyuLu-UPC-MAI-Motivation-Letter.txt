Motivation Letter
Feiyu Lu | UPC Master in Artificial Intelligence

I am applying to the interuniversity Master in Artificial Intelligence offered by UPC, UB, and URV because I want to connect nine years of production engineering experience with a stronger foundation in machine learning, intelligent systems, and responsible AI deployment. My career has not been a traditional AI-research path; it has been built around the infrastructure that modern AI depends on: reliable data movement, scalable services, low-latency analytics, observability, governance, and systems that remain correct under heavy load.

My interest in data and machine learning began during my junior year at Tufts University, where I earned a B.S. in Computer Science. As a Data Scientist Intern at DigitasLBi, I used Python and Spark with TF-IDF, Word2Vec, and K-Means to cluster more than 50,000 search phrases for campaign analysis. That project showed me how algorithmic methods can convert unstructured user behavior into practical decision support.

Since then, I have worked on much larger systems. At Amazon Advertising, I designed Java microservices and event-driven AWS pipelines that processed millions of telemetry and billing events per day. Building those systems required careful handling of concurrency, retries, idempotency, observability, testing, and global reliability. The lesson I took from that work is that AI applications are only useful when the surrounding platform is trustworthy.

At ExodusPoint Capital Management, I built real-time PnL and risk infrastructure for fixed income and equities using Java, Kafka, Apache Druid, WebSocket, and Protobuf. I optimized concurrent Java services and in-memory Druid workflows generating 100GB+ position snapshots per minute while reducing p99 query latency by 50% during market spikes. That experience made me interested in AI for finance, not as a superficial prediction layer, but as part of a rigorous decision system constrained by latency, data quality, and model reliability.

In my current role at Amazon Web Services, I work on distributed log ingestion and observability infrastructure. I have optimized Kinesis provisioning and ingestion throttling, reduced authorization latency through caching, and supported AI observability workflows for production GenAI services. As organizations deploy more AI systems, monitoring, responsible operation, and governance are becoming first-class technical problems.

The MAI curriculum fits this direction because it combines practical techniques with theoretical background. I am especially interested in Introduction to Machine Learning, Computational Intelligence, Planning and Approximate Reasoning, Big Data Analytics, Deep Learning, Reinforcement Learning, Probabilistic Graphical Models, Intelligent Data Analysis Applications in Business, and Responsible Artificial Intelligence. I would like to use the program to build deeper judgment about when AI methods are appropriate, how to evaluate them, and how to deploy them safely in production settings.

After the program, my goal is to become a senior technical leader or architect building AI-enabled infrastructure for cloud and financial technology organizations. I would bring the program a systems engineer's perspective: I understand how high-volume platforms fail, how users depend on operational correctness, and why AI must be connected to reliable data and observability. MAI would help me turn that experience into a stronger foundation for intelligent, scalable, and responsible systems.
